The iron ring is something students receive during their final year of a B.Eng. program in Canada. I got mine in 2018. About 8 years ago. And I've since been wearing it.

What is the purpose of wearing the iron ring? I can't remember. 

I also can't remember much from the Calling of an Engineer ritual, except maybe that we had to hold onto some giant cold iron chains while giving our oaths, which I thought was fun.

But what did I actually say in those oaths? 

I am sure they were about conduct and integrity, which I've seen so many people broke over my career, engineers or not engineers. But the details? Gone.

We have a summer studnet in the lab working towards her B.Eng., and last week we ended up talking about all this because I thought she was about to receive her ring. 

I even told her "Oaths (not just this oath, but any oaths) are designed to be forgotten once you've given them."

I still remember her face immediately went "Whoa that's such a lie".

Good for her, honestly.😂

But here's the thing, if oaths are to be remembered, why are they designed to be so lengthy?

In fact, I think oaths only serve 1 purpose, and that's that in the moment you take them, you take them very seriouesly and really mean what you have to say.

In that sense, yeah, they are designed to fade. And I think it is totally fine.

So, if none of these are here to stay, why do I still urge you to wear the ring? 

Is it so you can now brag about your engineering degree? No. That's what everybody does, you don't need that from me.

Is it because it looks stylish and goes well with your outfit? No, also not it, even if it happens to be true.

Is it because it sits on your pinky and reminds you about your obligation as an engineer? Well, that's... better, but still not quite exactly why I am pushing this so hard. 

Here is the real reason. If you hold yourself to certain standards, it's genuinely hard to find people like you, and the iron ring helps the right people find each other. 

And finding like-minded people is its own kind of joy. It sparks ideas, and reminds you that you're not fighting alone. 

You might think, OK, sure, well that's for people who have high standards - not me. 

But that's not true. 

From my experience, I can tell you very responsibly, that there are two kinds of people who have high standards. 

The first are the ones born with it. I've met plenty in my life and have become good firends with some of them. Working alongside them feels awesome and very motivating.

The second kind of people are trained to be like that. Yes, as surprising as it may seem, you can also be trained to have high standards. 

And I am pretty sure I belong to this second group. 

For me, it is the engineering education plus the influence of people from that first group that shaped me who I am. And despite things can be hard sometimes, I came out the other side as a better person, at least with a bit of standards and integrity to show for it.

So I wanted to put this out here, mostly to incentify those who are walking the same path right now (completing their B.Eng. and about to receive the ring) to join this club.

The world only becomes so much better if you (yes you, you who is reading this post right now, I don't care about others - others will always do what they will end up doing) choose to become someone with standards and integrity. 

And if you do, please don't forget to wear the ring to help me spot you out there.
